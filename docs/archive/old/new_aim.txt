8. But what do you mean by "reason"?

This is probably the most important reality check.

A neural network does not automatically know how to say:

"Supplier is risky because its risk increased 35%."

You have to build an explanation layer.

There are several ways to do that.

Option A — Rule-based explanation

This is the easiest and safest for a mini project.

The model predicts:

Supplier = 0.82

Then your explanation engine examines:

recent risk trend
rate of change
node's current risk
upstream/downstream exposure
contribution of other nodes

and generates:

"High risk primarily due to a strong upward trend in supplier risk."

This is very feasible.

9. Option B — Gradient-based explanation

More technically impressive.

Ask:

"Which input features caused this prediction to change the most?"

For example:

Supplier risk       ███████████████  45%
Manufacturer risk   ███████          22%
Recent trend        █████             16%
Total cost          ███               9%
Other                ██               8%

Then your explanation can say:

"The prediction was influenced most strongly by the recent supplier-risk values and manufacturer risk."

Your existing project plan already considers gradient-based attribution as an explainability extension.

13. There is another important issue: What exactly are we predicting?

This is where I want you to be careful.

The paper's continuous target is not mathematically specified very clearly.

Your current plan resolves that by defining:

$$ TRI = 0.25(RI_S+RI_M+RI_D+RI_R) $$

as an engineering interpretation of the overall risk target.

That's fine for the base-paper replication.

But if you're doing node-wise prediction, I'd rather define:

$$ Y = [R_S^{t+1},R_M^{t+1},R_D^{t+1},R_R^{t+1}] $$

Meaning:

Given the previous 10 time steps, predict the next risk of each node.

That's a much cleaner ML problem.

27. The biggest technical challenge

The hardest part will not be writing GCN or LSTM.

Those are relatively straightforward.

The hardest parts are:

1. Correct target construction

What exactly does:

"node risk"

mean?

I recommend:

$$ R_i(t+1) $$

= next-step risk of node \(i\).

2. Temporal leakage

You must never allow future information into the past.

Your existing plan correctly uses chronological splitting and train-only scaling.

3. Dynamic graph features

Don't accidentally give the GCN the same fixed node vector for every sample.

Your existing plan explicitly addresses this:

$$ X_{graph}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times4\times1} $$

with each window receiving its actual node risks.

That is very important.

4. Explainability

Don't claim:

"The neural network knows the reason."

Instead say:

"We provide post-hoc feature and temporal attribution to identify factors associated with the prediction."

That's scientifically defensible.
